\textbf{Where it misses.} Table~\ref{tab:misses} splits the \nMissAll{} observed calls the union
misses. Most (\missUnnamedAll) reach a function the caller never names: special methods invoked
implicitly (operators, iteration, attribute hooks) and callbacks or functions reached through
library code. No call site in the caller names these callees, so a resolver of call sites, as
our Pyright extractor and the LLMs are, cannot report them directly; the union still holds
\pctUnnamedFound{} of them. A resolver that modelled operator dispatch and library callbacks could
recover more. They explain the lowest recall: \texttt{python-lazy-object-proxy}
(\edgeUnionMin) is a proxy-object library whose tests exercise operator overloading, so almost every
observed call is a special method that no call site names. Method names shared by several
functions come next, with \missSharedAll{} of all misses and \pctMissShared{} of those whose callee
the caller names: dispatch over a class hierarchy or over unrelated classes with a common method
name. \texttt{ShivyC}, a C compiler, defines \texttt{make\_il} on \shivycMakeIl{} syntax-tree
classes and \texttt{make\_asm} on \shivycMakeAsm, and the union holds fewer than half of its
observed calls. Shared-name misses concentrate in a few large repositories: the median repository
loses \medMissCallShared{} of its misses to shared-name calls but \medMissUnnamedAll{} to unnamed ones taken together. Names written without call syntax, such as
property reads and functions passed as values, account for \missReferenceAll{} of misses.

\textbf{Not reflection.} Functions that use reflection (a \texttt{getattr}-family call with a
computed name, \texttt{eval} or \texttt{exec}, a dynamic import) make only \pctReflectCalls{} of
observed calls (\nReflectCalls). Their calls are missed at a rate of \reflectMissRate{} against \plainMissRate{}
elsewhere, and they account for \pctMissReflective{} of all misses. Across repositories, the
dynamism score used to stratify the corpus shows no evidence of a relation to recall (Spearman
$\rho = \densitySpearmanExact$, $p = \densitySpearmanP$; Fig.~\ref{fig:recall}b; without the
\nSmallRepos{} repositories with fewer than 20 observed calls, $\rho = \bigDensitySpearman$,
$p = \bigDensitySpearmanP$), and recall does not differ by stratum (Kruskal--Wallis
$p = \kwStrataP$; medians \edgeMedLow{} for LOW,
\edgeMedMed{} for MED and \edgeMedHigh{} for HIGH). Static analysers lose calls in this corpus to
implicit calls and dispatch over shared names, not to reflection.

\textbf{Coverage and stability of the labels.} The tests executed a median of \covMed{} of each
package's statement lines (\covMin{} to \covMax{}). Recall falls as coverage rises, a
marginal relation (Spearman $\rho = \covSpearman$, $p = \covSpearmanP$, 95\% CI $\covSpearmanCI$),
and shows no clear relation to the number of tests ($\rho = \testsSpearmanExact$, $p = \testsSpearmanP$).
If suites that reach more code also reach more hard calls, recall on lightly tested repositories
is overstated. \retraceSentence{}

\textbf{Does the seed change recall?} \sweepRecall{}}

\renewcommand{\rqFourBody}{%
On the sampled callers, which are enriched for hard items, every LLM finds more of the observed calls than static analysis does:
recall rises from \llmTwentyStaticR{} for the static union on the same items to \llmTwentyR{} for gpt-oss-20b, \llmOneTwentyR{} for gpt-oss-120b and \llmQwenR{} for qwen3.8-27b.
Precision falls from \llmTwentyStaticP{} to between \llmOneTwentyP{} and \llmQwenP: the models claim calls
the tests never made. The larger gpt-oss model has the highest recall and the lowest precision.
On real code, then, the micro-benchmark finding that PyCG outperforms LLMs~\cite{venkatesh2025llmcg}
holds for precision but reverses for recall, on a narrower task: our models choose among names on
executed lines rather than build a graph. With whole repositories resampled, the 95\% interval for
recall is \ciOneTwentyRec{} for gpt-oss-120b against \ciStaticRec{} for the static union, but the F1
intervals overlap (\ciOneTwentyFone{} against \ciStaticFone{}), and an LLM has the higher F1 in only
\llmBeatsMin--\llmBeatsMax{} of \llmReposN{} repositories. Calls the caller never names are not
items, so this comparison covers only named calls. The sample holds \nSampleCallers{} callers, and
\sampleBaseRate{} of its items are observed calls. Only \nSampleStaticOnly{} callers entered it
solely through a static error; without them, static precision and recall are \woStaticP{} and
\woStaticR{} and the LLMs' \woLlmP{} and \woLlmR, so the comparison does not rest on that
criterion.

The LLMs complement static analysis rather than duplicate it: on observed calls the static union
missed they recover \compMissedR{}, and on those it found they agree on \compFoundR{}.
The gain comes from hard names. Every resolver finds almost all calls to uniquely
named functions. On shared method names static recall is \kindSharedStaticR{} against
\kindSharedLlmR{} for the LLMs. Names written without call syntax are almost absent from static
graphs (recall at most \kindRefStaticR); the LLMs recover them at \kindRefLlmR{}, but with precision
of only \kindRefLlmP.

\renewcommand{\rqDiscussionLlm}{Static analysis is precise but blind to dispatch over shared
names and to references; the LLMs see those but over-claim, and neither sees implicit calls, the
largest class of misses. Neither is enough alone; combined,
whether by a learned filter or by a plain union of Pyright and the best LLM, they give a small F1
gain on unseen repositories
(\loroUnionFone{} to \loroCombFone). A tool that
consumes call graphs can treat LLM answers as a second vote, not as a replacement.}

\renewcommand{\rqConclusion}{the union of two static analysers finds a median of \edgeUnionMed{} of
the calls per repository (\poolRecallUnion{} pooled). Most of what it misses are implicit calls that
no call site names, then calls through shared method names; reflection accounts for
\pctMissReflective. LLMs recover many of the named misses at a cost in precision,
and combining the two, whether by a learned filter or a plain union, gives only a small F1 gain on
unseen repositories (\loroUnionFone{} to \loroCombFone).}
